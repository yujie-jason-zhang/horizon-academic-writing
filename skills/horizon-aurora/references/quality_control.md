# Quality Control

## Failure Signals

Targeted repair is required when:

1. prose is fluent but the scientific point is still hard to recover;
2. paragraphs are locally polished but the section lacks a stable progression;
3. cohesion is under-signaled where a genuine relation exists;
4. cohesion is over-signaled through unnecessary transitions;
5. directness improves but claim strength, scope, causality, uncertainty, or a necessary limitation drifts;
6. concision removes an important condition, number, comparison, or qualification;
7. the same result is repeatedly repackaged as a new major finding;
8. important claims change strength or scope across sections without scientific reason;
9. formulaic academic phrasing, decorative symmetry, or repetitive cadence dominates nearby prose;
10. terminology, notation, or important numerical conditions conflict in ways that affect interpretation.

## Final Reader Test

Before delivery, ask:

1. What did this section accomplish?
2. Does each important paragraph have a clear function?
3. Can the main action be found without unnecessary rereading?
4. Does new information have enough context?
5. Are adjacent relations signaled enough but not excessively?
6. Is the supported point stated directly without deleting required caution?
7. Are lexical choices natural, precise, and evidence-proportional?
8. Is any sentence harder than the scientific idea requires?
9. Are functional parallel structures preserved while decorative repetition is reduced?
10. Do major claims retain consistent scope and strength across sections?
11. Did any edit change technical meaning, agency, causality, uncertainty, numerical binding, or citation scope?

If 10 or 11 fails, repair is mandatory.

Verify the final delivery files against the source after all repairs. Earlier checker results do not cover subsequent edits. Confirm completed section/project coverage and manually review custom macro arguments beyond the checker's declared support. Treat original style issues as advisories rather than reasons to alter protected source.

## Material Edit Notes

Report only edits that deserve author attention, such as:

- claim-bearing language whose equivalence deserves review;
- paragraph/cross-paragraph reordering;
- citation movement;
- numerical-statement repositioning;
- a terminology choice made under source ambiguity.

Do not report routine grammar and punctuation edits individually.
